# CTE Teacher Alignment & Collaboration Protocol
**Corvallis School District 509j: CVHS & CHS Cross-Campus Collaboration**

> **Purpose:** As our district navigates enrollment contraction and population shifts, our CTE programs must evolve from isolated campus offerings into a unified, district-wide engine of economic opportunity for students. This document is a structured framework for CVHS and CHS CTE teachers to collaboratively evaluate, align, and future-proof their pathways without the friction of "turf wars."

---

## 1. The Mindset: System-Level Thinking
When teachers meet to align courses, the goal is not to force one campus's curriculum onto another. The goal is to build a unified **Scope and Sequence** that serves students across the city.
*   **The Shared Reality:** We have limited FTE and physical space. Redundant, under-enrolled introductory courses cost us the ability to run high-level, advanced capstone courses.
*   **The Mutual Goal:** Maximize student transitions into high-wage, high-demand careers, and drastically expand our **Linn-Benton College Now** dual-credit articulations.

---

## 2. The Meeting Protocol: "Data Over Defensiveness"
When CTE PLC (Professional Learning Community) groups convene across CVHS and CHS, discussions must strictly adhere to the following framework:

### Phase A: Syllabus & Skill Deconstruction (The 60% Rule)
*   **Action:** Bring your current syllabus, project rubrics, and industry standards list.
*   **Analysis:** If a CVHS course and a CHS course share **60% or more of their foundational skills**, they should mathematically share the same course title, description, and prerequisites district-wide. 
*   **The Question:** "Are we teaching the same foundational skills under different names?"

### Phase B: Vertical Alignment & The "Bottleneck" Check
*   **Action:** Map your courses onto a 4-year high school trajectory.
*   **Analysis:** Do we have too many introductory (Level 1) offerings and not enough Level 3/4 capstones?
*   **The Question:** "If a student takes my class as a sophomore, what is their exact next step as a junior? If there is no next step, why does the class exist?"

### Phase C: The "College Now" Gap Analysis
*   **Action:** Compare current course outcomes to the Linn-Benton Community College (LBCC) CTE introductory course syllabi.
*   **Analysis:** Why isn't this course currently articulated for College Now credit? Is it a rigor issue, a teacher certification issue, or just an administrative gap we haven't crossed yet?
*   **The Question:** "What 20% of my curriculum do I need to change to secure a dual-credit articulation agreement by next fall?"

---

## 3. Tangible Alignment Deliverables (What Teachers Must Produce)
By the end of the alignment summit, the cross-campus CTE teams must produce the following for District Leadership:

1.  **A Unified District Course Catalog Draft:** Consolidated course descriptions that apply to both CHS and CVHS.
2.  **The Divest/Invest Proposal:** A joint recommendation of at least ONE legacy course to sunset, and ONE emerging technology/industry skill to integrate into the newly freed instructional time.
3.  **The Capstone Hub Agreement:** A proposal denoting which campus has the physical infrastructure to host the "Advanced Capstone" for specific pathways (e.g., "Intro to Engineering" at both campuses, but "Advanced Robotics Fabrication" hosted exclusively at CVHS).

---

## 4. Measuring Success at the Classroom Level
We will know our alignment is successful based on the following metrics:
*   **Metric 1:** A 30% increase in students earning LBCC "College Now" dual-credit in CTE pathways over the next 24 months.
*   **Metric 2:** Elimination of all courses categorized as "Orphans" (courses with no Level 2 follow-up) by the next academic scheduling cycle.
*   **Metric 3:** A 15% increase in cross-campus enrollment (students utilizing district transport to take an advanced CTE capstone at the sister high school).
